What is Physics?

Physics is all about understanding how the universe works. It explores the rules that govern everything around us, from falling objects and electricity to atoms, planets, and distant galaxies. The subject covers many branches, including mechanics, thermodynamics, electromagnetism, quantum physics, and astrophysics, each focusing on different aspects of nature. Physics moves forward through a combination of experiments and theory: experiments help us observe and test the world, while theories help explain what we see and predict new discoveries. For centuries, curious and creative thinkers have pushed the boundaries of our knowledge, helping us better understand the universe and develop technologies that have transformed everyday life.

Branches of Physics

Mechanics is the study of motion and forces. It explains why objects move, stop, speed up, or change direction. From a ball being thrown through the air to the motion of planets around the Sun, mechanics helps us understand how things move in the world around us.

Thermodynamics is the study of heat, energy, and temperature. It helps explain how engines work, why ice melts, and how energy flows from one place to another. This branch of physics plays an important role in technology, weather, and many everyday processes.

Electromagnetism explores electricity, magnetism, and their connection. It explains how electric circuits, motors, generators, and many modern devices work. Without electromagnetism, we would not have electrical power, comp#8B0000uters, phones, or many of the technologies we rely on every day.

Optics is the study of light and how it behaves. It examines reflection, refraction, colour, and vision. Optics helps us understand how we see the world and is used in cameras, glasses, microscopes, telescopes, and many other tools that use light.

Acoustics is the study of sound and vibrations. It explains how sounds are produced, travel through materials, and are heard by our ears. Acoustics is important in music, communication, architecture, and technologies such as speakers, microphones, and ultrasound devices.

Quantum physics studies the tiny particles that make up the universe, such as atoms and electrons. At these very small scales, nature behaves in surprising ways that differ from everyday experience. Quantum physics is essential to modern technology, including computers, lasers, and electronic devices.

Atomic physics focuses on the structure and behaviour of atoms. It investigates how electrons move around atomic nuclei and how atoms interact with light and energy. This branch helps scientists understand the building blocks of matter and supports many modern technologies.

Nuclear physics studies the nuclei found at the centre of atoms. It explores radioactivity, nuclear energy, and the forces that hold nuclei together. This field contributes to energy production, medical imaging, cancer treatments, and our understanding of how stars generate energy.

Particle physics investigates the smallest known particles and the forces that act between them. Scientists use powerful experiments to explore what matter is made of and how the universe works at its most fundamental level. It seeks answers to some of science's biggest questions.

Astrophysics applies the laws of physics to objects in space. It studies stars, planets, galaxies, black holes, and the universe itself. By observing the cosmos, astrophysicists learn how celestial objects form, evolve, and interact across vast distances.
